After this page you will be able to tune how windows move when the layout changes, and you
will know when turning animation off is the better choice.

## What is animated

When the layout changes, windows glide to their new place instead of jumping. That happens
when a window opens or closes, when you move, join or flip, when you resize with a shortcut,
when you zoom, and so on. A floating window you put somewhere with
[`place`](/reference/commands/#place) glides there too.

Some things are never animated:

- **Arriving on another desktop.** Layout changes that happen as you arrive are applied
  instantly, so you do not watch windows shuffle after the system's own slide.
- **Anything while Reduce Motion is on.** If macOS's Reduce Motion setting is on, Iguana's
  animation is off, whatever the config says.
- **A window that has only just joined the layout.** A newly opened window is put in its
  slot at once. The windows that make room for it glide. A new floating window that a
  [rule](/guides/floating-windows/#size-and-place-an-apps-windows) sizes or places is also put
  there at once.
- **Very small changes.** A change of 12 points or less is applied at once.

<Aside>
The slide between desktops is a different animation. It belongs to macOS, and these settings
do not affect it. See [Desktops and displays](/guides/desktops-and-displays/#switch-desktop).
</Aside>

## Settings

```toml title="iguana.toml"
[animation]
enabled = true       # windows glide to their new place instead of jumping
duration = 0.16      # seconds, 0.05 to 1
easing = "ease-out"  # "linear", "ease-out" or "ease-in-out"
```

| Key | Default | Allowed | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `enabled` | `true` | `true`, `false` | Whether windows glide or jump. |
| `duration` | `0.16` | 0.05 to 1 | Seconds a window takes to reach its new place. |
| `easing` | `"ease-out"` | `"linear"`, `"ease-out"`, `"ease-in-out"` | How the motion is paced. |

The easings:

- `linear`: constant speed from start to finish.
- `ease-out`: starts fast and slows into place.
- `ease-in-out`: starts slowly, speeds up, and slows into place.

The same settings are in the **Animation** pane of the
[settings window](/guides/settings-window/#animation), where `enabled` is
**Animate window changes**, `duration` is **Duration** and `easing` is **Pacing**. The pane
also has a **Play a move** button that plays a move in a miniature of your desktop, so you
can try a duration and pacing before your real windows use them.

## How it stays smooth

- **Frames are paced by the display's refresh.** Iguana produces one step of the motion per
  refresh of the display.
- **Each app is driven on its own background queue.** A slow app cannot hold up the windows
  of the others.
- **Frames are dropped, not queued.** When an app cannot keep up, Iguana skips intermediate
  frames instead of letting them pile up.
- **Every window ends exactly on its final frame**, however many steps were skipped on the way.

## Why some apps look choppy

macOS gives one app no way to transform another app's window. Iguana cannot slide a picture
of the window across the screen. For every step of the animation it has to ask the app to
actually move and resize its window, and the app has to redraw its contents at the new size.

Light apps redraw quickly and look smooth. Heavy apps, such as browsers and Electron apps,
redraw more slowly and may look choppier.

If that bothers you:

- **Shorten the duration.** A short animation has fewer steps for a slow app to miss.

  ```toml title="iguana.toml"
  [animation]
  duration = 0.08
  ```

- **Turn animation off.** Windows then jump straight to their new place, which is always
  crisp.

  ```toml title="iguana.toml"
  [animation]
  enabled = false
  ```
